PMV Evaluation

PMV Evaluation

Work through each evaluation scenario as though you are the treating Speech-Language Pathologist. At each step, determine whether you would continue, modify, or discontinue the trial.

Scenario 1: Initial PMV Trial

Step 1: Baseline Assessment

Review Jane’s baseline status before beginning the evaluation.

Measure Value
HR 82 bpm
RR 18 breaths/min
SpO₂ 94%
FiO₂ 28% via humidified trach collar
Alertness Awake, alert, and cooperative
Secretions Moderate; able to cough and clear independently

Clinical Decision

  1. Does Jane appear medically and respiratory stable enough to begin the evaluation?
  2. What additional information should be confirmed before proceeding?
  3. Would you proceed with cuff deflation?

Step 2: Cuff Deflation

Respiratory Therapy confirms that the cuff is completely deflated.

Jane coughs several times following cuff deflation and clears a small amount of thin secretions. After two minutes, her respiratory status remains stable.

Measure Value
HR 84 bpm
RR 19 breaths/min
SpO₂ 94%

Clinical Decision

  1. Is this response expected following cuff deflation?
  2. Would you continue with assessment of upper-airway patency?
  3. What signs would cause you to stop at this point?

Step 3: Upper-Airway Patency

With brief digital occlusion, Jane produces a weak but audible voice.

Airflow is noted through the mouth and nose. Jane reports that breathing feels slightly more difficult but denies significant distress.

Clinical Decision

  1. Why is audible voice with digital occlusion clinically important?
  2. What does this suggest about upper-airway patency?
  3. Would you proceed with PMV placement?

Step 4: PMV Placement

The PMV is placed.

During the first minute, Jane produces several short verbal responses. Her voice is breathy but audible.

After approximately three minutes, Jane begins to demonstrate increased work of breathing.

Measure Baseline After 3 Minutes
HR 82 bpm 94 bpm
RR 18 breaths/min 26 breaths/min
SpO₂ 94% 89%
Work of Breathing Comfortable Increased
Patient Report No concern “It feels harder to breathe.”

Jane appears mildly anxious and begins using accessory muscles for breathing.

Clinical Decision

  1. Is Jane tolerating the PMV?
  2. What findings indicate poor tolerance?
  3. What should the SLP do immediately?
  4. Which team member should be notified?
  5. What possible factors may have contributed to poor tolerance?

Jane’s SpO₂ improves to 93% shortly after the PMV is removed. Her respiratory rate and work of breathing return toward baseline with rest.

Initial Clinical Impression

Discuss with your group:

  • Is Jane an appropriate candidate for continued PMV use today?
  • Would you discontinue the evaluation entirely or consider reassessment later?
  • What recommendations would you document following this trial?
  • What additional medical or respiratory information may be helpful before another attempt?

Scenario 2: PMV Reassessment

Two days later, Jane is referred for reassessment.

Respiratory Therapy reports improved secretion management and increased tolerance of daytime trach collar trials. Jane has remained medically stable and continues to request an opportunity to speak.

Step 1: Updated Baseline Status

Measure Value
HR 78 bpm
RR 17 breaths/min
SpO₂ 95%
FiO₂ 28% via humidified trach collar
Alertness Awake, alert, and cooperative
Secretions Mild; independently managed
Cuff Status Completely deflated
Respiratory Effort Comfortable at rest

Clinical Decision

  1. What has changed since the initial evaluation?
  2. Do these findings support another PMV trial?
  3. What precautions should remain in place?

Step 2: Repeat PMV Trial

The PMV is placed following confirmation of cuff deflation and upper-airway patency.

Jane wears the valve for 10 minutes while seated upright.

Measure Baseline After 10 Minutes
HR 78 bpm 82 bpm
RR 17 breaths/min 19 breaths/min
SpO₂ 95% 94%
Work of Breathing Comfortable Comfortable
Voice Weak Audible and functional
Secretions Mild Managed independently

Jane communicates in short sentences and reports:

“It feels much easier today.”

No significant respiratory distress, air trapping, or oxygen desaturation is observed.

Clinical Decision

  1. Is Jane tolerating the PMV during this trial?
  2. What objective and subjective findings support your decision?
  3. Would you recommend immediate independent use?
  4. What level of supervision is appropriate?
  5. How should wear time be advanced?

Develop Your Recommendations

Create a preliminary PMV plan for Jane.

Consider including:

  • Recommended wear schedule
  • Level of supervision
  • Appropriate activities for initial use
  • Respiratory monitoring
  • Cuff precautions
  • Patient and staff education
  • Criteria for removing the valve
  • Need for continued SLP and Respiratory Therapy follow-up

Example Recommendation Categories

Students may consider whether Jane should initially wear the PMV:

  • During Speech-Language Pathology sessions
  • During Physical and Occupational Therapy
  • During supervised communication with family
  • During nursing care when trained staff are present
  • During a future swallowing evaluation, if clinically appropriate

Final Questions

  1. Would you recommend PMV use during therapies?
  2. Would you recommend a specific initial wear duration?
  3. What must staff verify before every placement?
  4. What symptoms should prompt immediate valve removal?
  5. What criteria should be met before progressing toward longer or less-supervised use?
