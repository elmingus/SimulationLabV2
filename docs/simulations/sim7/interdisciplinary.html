Interdisciplinary Notes

Interdisciplinary Documentation

Review documentation from the healthcare team before proceeding with Karla’s Passy-Muir Valve and swallowing evaluation.

Respiratory Therapy

08:10

Trach: Shiley 6 cuffed tracheostomy with deflatable cuff

  • Patient remains off mechanical ventilation during daytime hours.
  • Receiving humidified tracheostomy collar at 28% FiO₂.
  • Resting SpO₂ ranges from 94–96%.
  • Cuff deflation has been completed for brief periods without significant oxygen desaturation.
  • Moderate thin secretions present; patient demonstrates a productive cough and is able to clear secretions with minimal assistance.
  • Mild increase in respiratory rate noted with prolonged activity, resolving with rest.
  • No obvious evidence of acute respiratory distress at rest.
  • Respiratory Therapy available to assist with cuff deflation and initial PMV placement.

Nursing

08:45

Patient is awake and cooperative with care.

Oriented to person and place. Intermittent confusion noted regarding date and recent hospital events.

Communicates through mouthing words, gestures, and writing. Frequently attempts to vocalize.

Patient remains NPO and is receiving enteral nutrition through a nasogastric tube.

Nursing reports that family has repeatedly asked when Karla will be able to eat and drink again.

Daughter reports that Karla had been “eating less and coughing more” for several weeks prior to admission.

No acute respiratory distress observed this morning.

Internal Medicine

09:00

Patient continues to improve following severe community-acquired pneumonia complicated by acute hypoxemic respiratory failure.

Status post prolonged intubation and subsequent tracheostomy placement.

Currently tolerating daytime tracheostomy collar and remains hemodynamically stable.

Continue pulmonary rehabilitation and progressive mobility.

Patient remains NPO pending Speech-Language Pathology evaluation.

Given reports of coughing with meals and unintentional weight loss prior to hospitalization, swallowing concerns may not be solely related to recent intubation or tracheostomy placement.

Proceed with SLP evaluation as tolerated.

Dietitian

09:20

Patient currently receiving enteral nutrition through nasogastric tube.

Chart review and family interview indicate approximately 8-pound unintentional weight loss over the past two months.

Daughter reports declining oral intake prior to hospitalization, with patient frequently leaving meals unfinished.

Current estimated nutritional needs are being met through enteral feeding.

Recommend continued monitoring of nutritional status and coordination with SLP regarding readiness for oral intake.

Physical Therapy

Previous Day - 14:30

Patient transferred from bed to chair with contact guard assistance and ambulated approximately 75 feet with rolling walker.

Mild shortness of breath noted following activity.

SpO₂ remained above 92% throughout session.

Patient followed simple commands appropriately but benefited from occasional repetition.

Overall endurance remains reduced compared with baseline.

Clinical Reasoning

Discuss the following with your group before proceeding:

  1. Which findings support proceeding with a PMV evaluation today?

  2. What factors should be monitored closely during cuff deflation and PMV placement?

  3. Which findings increase concern for dysphagia independent of the tracheostomy?

  4. How do Karla’s weight loss and reduced oral intake influence your interpretation of the case?

  5. How might prolonged intubation and respiratory deconditioning further affect swallowing function?

  6. Which disciplines provide information that is most relevant to your evaluation plan?

  7. Based on the available information, what should occur first: PMV evaluation, swallowing evaluation, or both? Explain your reasoning.

Do not assume that successful PMV placement indicates safe swallowing. PMV tolerance and swallowing function must be evaluated separately while considering Karla’s respiratory status, medical history, and pre-hospital symptoms.
