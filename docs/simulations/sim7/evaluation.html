Clinical Evaluation

Karla Green — Bedside Evaluation

You are now seeing Karla at bedside for her Passy-Muir Valve and clinical swallowing evaluation.

Use the patient, available equipment, medical record, and information obtained from the healthcare team to complete your assessment.

SLP Task

Conduct the evaluation as you would in the acute care setting.

Determine what information you need, obtain appropriate baseline measures, complete the PMV assessment, and proceed with the clinical swallowing evaluation as appropriate.

Do not assume that all necessary information will be provided automatically. Ask questions, obtain measurements, and request additional findings when clinically indicated.

Entering the Room

Karla is resting in bed with the head of bed elevated.

She is awake and visually attends when you enter the room.

She currently has:

  • Shiley 6 cuffed tracheostomy
  • Humidified tracheostomy collar at 28% FiO₂
  • Nasogastric tube in place
  • Pulse oximeter available at bedside
  • Writing materials available for communication

Karla attempts to mouth a greeting when you approach.

Patient Interview

Before beginning the physical assessment, obtain information directly from Karla as appropriate.

What would you like to know?

Develop your own interview questions.

Consider what information may be relevant to:

  • Current symptoms
  • Swallowing prior to hospitalization
  • Current swallowing concerns
  • Previous diet
  • Eating and drinking habits
  • Recent changes in intake
  • Respiratory symptoms
  • Communication
  • Patient goals

Ask the patient your questions. Your instructor will provide Karla’s responses.

Discuss

  1. Which information from Karla’s interview is most clinically relevant?

  2. Does the patient report align with information obtained from her daughter and the medical record?

  3. Is there additional information you would want from Karla’s family or healthcare team?

Oral Mechanism Examination

Complete a focused oral mechanism examination.

Plan Your Examination

Determine which structures and functions should be assessed based on Karla’s history and today’s referral.

Do not rely on the website as an OME checklist. Conduct the examination with your patient and request findings from your instructor as needed.

Consider information related to:

  • Facial symmetry and movement
  • Labial function
  • Lingual function
  • Jaw movement
  • Palatal movement
  • Dentition and oral condition
  • Secretion management
  • Volitional cough
  • Voice, if obtainable

Clinical Reasoning

After completing the OME, discuss:

  1. Which findings were within functional limits?

  2. Which findings may influence swallowing efficiency or safety?

  3. Are any findings consistent with Karla’s medical history?

  4. Do the OME findings change how you plan to approach PO trials?

Passy-Muir Valve Evaluation

Determine whether Karla is appropriate to proceed with cuff deflation and PMV placement.

Apply What You Learned in Simulation 6

You are responsible for determining the appropriate sequence of the PMV evaluation.

Consider:

  • Tracheostomy characteristics
  • Cuff status
  • Secretion management
  • Upper-airway patency
  • Baseline respiratory status
  • Patient positioning
  • Patient response to cuff deflation
  • Respiratory status following valve placement

Verbalize each step of your assessment and request findings from your instructor as needed.

Clinical Swallowing Evaluation

If you determine that Karla is medically appropriate to continue, proceed with the swallowing portion of the evaluation.

Plan Your PO Trials

Available consistencies may include:

  • Ice chips
  • Thin liquid
  • Mildly thick liquid
  • Puree
  • Soft solid
  • Regular solid

You are responsible for selecting the consistencies, method of presentation, amount, and progression of PO trials.

Explain your rationale before administering each trial.

Before Each Trial

Consider:

  • Patient positioning
  • Alertness
  • Respiratory status
  • Secretion management
  • Oral readiness
  • Appropriate bolus size
  • Method of presentation

During PO Trials

Observe for clinical findings related to:

Oral Phase

  • Acceptance
  • Labial seal
  • Bolus control
  • Mastication
  • Oral manipulation
  • Oral transit
  • Oral residue

Pharyngeal / Airway-Related Clinical Signs

  • Coughing
  • Throat clearing
  • Changes in vocal quality
  • Multiple swallows
  • Changes in respiratory pattern
  • Increased work of breathing
  • Patient-reported difficulty

Overall Tolerance

  • Endurance
  • Fatigue
  • Ability to maintain attention
  • Ability to follow directions
  • Changes across repeated trials

Continue, Modify, or Stop?

You are not required to administer every available consistency.

After each portion of the evaluation, determine whether you have sufficient information to:

  • Continue PO trials
  • Modify the presentation or consistency
  • Trial an alternative consistency
  • Discontinue PO trials
  • Recommend additional assessment

Be prepared to justify your decision.

Reassessment

Before completing the bedside encounter:

  • Reassess respiratory status as indicated.
  • Obtain repeat vital signs as appropriate.
  • Assess patient comfort.
  • Consider secretion management.
  • Determine whether the PMV should remain in place.
  • Provide appropriate patient education.

Patient Education

What would you explain to Karla before leaving the room?

Consider:

  • Findings from today’s evaluation
  • PMV use
  • Current swallowing recommendations
  • Safety precautions
  • Need for additional assessment
  • Next steps in care

End of Bedside Evaluation

Before leaving the room, discuss with your group:

  1. What are the three most important findings from today’s evaluation?

  2. What findings increase your concern for oropharyngeal dysphagia?

  3. What findings support or limit continued PMV use?

  4. Do you have enough information to make an oral diet recommendation?

  5. What information remains unknown?

  6. Is an instrumental swallowing assessment indicated?

Remember

A clinical swallowing evaluation provides important information about swallowing function and clinical risk, but it does not allow direct visualization of swallowing physiology.

Your recommendations should reflect both what you observed and what remains unknown.
