Additional Acute Care Skills Resources

Use these resources to review the evidence and continue developing skills in dysphagia assessment and management.

Assigned Readings

Dysphagia Treatment

Duncan, S., McAuley, D. F., Walshe, M., McGaughey, J., Anand, R., Fallis, R., & Blackwood, B. (2020). Interventions for oropharyngeal dysphagia in acute and critical care: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Intensive Care Medicine, 46, 1326–1338.

View the article DOI

Review Questions

  1. What patient populations were represented in the review?
  2. Which outcomes demonstrated a statistically significant benefit?
  3. How strong was the certainty of evidence?
  4. Why are the results difficult to generalize to all ICU patients?
  5. Which interventions were identified as biologically plausible for critically ill patients?
  6. What patient-centered outcomes should future studies include?

Bedside Screening and Instrumentation

Bours, G. J. J. W., Speyer, R., Lemmens, J., Limburg, M., & de Wit, R. (2009). Bedside screening tests versus videofluoroscopy or fibreoptic endoscopic evaluation of swallowing to detect dysphagia in patients with neurological disorders: Systematic review. Journal of Advanced Nursing, 65(3), 477–493.

View the article DOI

Review Questions

  1. What is the difference between a screening test and a diagnostic assessment?
  2. Why were the study results not statistically pooled?
  3. Which bedside findings performed poorly when used alone?
  4. Why is high sensitivity important in dysphagia screening?
  5. What limitations should be considered when applying this review today?

FEES Learning

FEES competency requires formal education, direct instruction, supervised practice, and demonstration of clinical competence.

Students should not independently perform FEES based solely on observation of today’s demonstration.

Potential areas for continued learning include:

  • FEES indications and contraindications
  • Endoscopic anatomy
  • Scope handling
  • Nasal passage
  • Secretion rating
  • Penetration-Aspiration Scale scoring
  • Residue rating
  • Interpretation of white-out
  • Medical emergency procedures
  • Infection prevention
  • Documentation
  • Billing and institutional credentialing

Simulation 5 Takeaways

  • A focused oral mechanism examination contributes important information but does not independently determine swallowing safety.
  • Single clinical findings should not be interpreted in isolation.
  • Bedside assessment and instrumental assessment answer different clinical questions.
  • FEES and MBSS each provide unique information.
  • Dysphagia treatment should target an identified impairment or functional problem.
  • Evidence for specific treatments in acute and critical care remains limited.
  • Medical stability, cognition, endurance, and patient goals must guide treatment planning.
  • Clinical recommendations should clearly distinguish observed findings from clinical inference.
